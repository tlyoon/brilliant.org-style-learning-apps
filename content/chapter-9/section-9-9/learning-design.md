# 9.9 Rocket Propulsion learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.9 Rocket Propulsion: Momentum conservation in variable-mass isolated systems; Rocket thrust as the product of exhaust speed and burn rate; Rocket propulsion equation involving mass ratio logarithm; Exhaust velocity relative to the rocket versus inertial frame; Uniform center of mass velocity in free space. Excludes: Relativistic rocket dynamics; Atmospheric drag and aerodynamic lift effects; Gravitational potential variations across orbital trajectories; Multi-stage staging optimization mathematics.

## Learning objectives

1. Model a rocket and its expelled exhaust as an isolated system conserving total linear momentum.
2. Explain rocket thrust as an internal reaction force without requiring external air to push against.
3. Analyze how exhaust speed and mass ratio determine the final speed change of a rocket.
4. Relate thrust to exhaust speed and fuel burn rate using Newton's second and third laws.
5. Describe the uniform motion of the system center of mass in free space during propulsion.

## Prerequisites

- `conservation-of-momentum`: Understanding that total linear momentum is constant in an isolated system.
- `newtons-third-law`: Recognizing that forces between interacting objects are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
- `center-of-mass-motion`: Understanding that internal forces do not accelerate the center of mass of a system.

## Misconception catalogue

- `needs-atmosphere-to-push`: Believing that rockets require external air or a physical medium to push against to accelerate.
- `exhaust-pushes-rocket-externally`: Viewing exhaust gas as an external applied force rather than an internal reaction from momentum ejection.
- `constant-mass-assumption`: Treating the accelerating rocket as having constant mass, ignoring the continuous loss of propellant.
- `thrust-equals-rocket-speed`: Confusing rocket forward speed with the exhaust speed relative to the rocket.
- `linear-speed-gain-with-fuel`: Believing that final rocket velocity increases linearly with fuel mass rather than logarithmically with mass ratio.
