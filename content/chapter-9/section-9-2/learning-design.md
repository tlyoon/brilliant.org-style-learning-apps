# 9.2 Analysis Model: Isolated System (Momentum) learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.2 Analysis Model: Isolated System (Momentum): Definition and criteria of an isolated system for linear momentum; Total momentum vector constancy (delta p_tot = 0); Independent momentum conservation along Cartesian axes (x, y, z); Distinction between internal interaction forces and external net forces; Recoil dynamics in isolated multi-particle systems. Excludes: Nonisolated systems and impulse-momentum theorem calculations; Elastic and inelastic collision classification and coefficient of restitution; Center of mass dynamics and multi-particle kinematics; Variable-mass systems and rocket thrust propulsion equations.

## Learning objectives

1. Define an isolated system in the context of linear momentum as one subject to zero net external force.
2. State and apply the principle of conservation of linear momentum for an isolated system.
3. Analyze momentum conservation independently along orthogonal coordinate axes.
4. Distinguish between internal forces that exchange momentum and external forces that alter system momentum.
5. Predict recoil velocities of interacting bodies within an isolated system using directional conservation.

## Prerequisites

- `linear-momentum-definition`: Definition of linear momentum as the vector product of mass and velocity (p = mv).
- `newtons-third-law`: Newton's third law stating interaction forces between two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
- `vector-components`: Resolution of vectors into independent orthogonal components (x, y, z).

## Misconception catalogue

- `single-particle-conservation`: Believing that the momentum of every individual particle within an isolated system must remain constant.
- `internal-force-confusion`: Assuming that internal forces between interacting system members can change the total momentum of the system.
- `scalar-momentum-treatment`: Treating momentum as a scalar quantity by neglecting directional signs and component independence during interactions.
- `kinetic-energy-momentum-conflation`: Confusing kinetic energy with momentum, expecting total kinetic energy to remain constant whenever momentum is conserved.
- `vertical-force-invalidates-horizontal-conservation`: Believing that external vertical forces (such as gravity or normal force) prevent momentum conservation along an isolated horizontal axis.
