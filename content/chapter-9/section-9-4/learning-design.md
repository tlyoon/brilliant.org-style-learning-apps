# 9.4 Collisions in One Dimension learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.4 Collisions in One Dimension: Definition and categorization of elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions; Conservation of linear momentum in one-dimensional isolated collisions; Conservation of kinetic energy in one-dimensional elastic collisions; Relative speed of approach equals relative speed of separation for 1D elastic collisions; Special limiting mass cases for 1D elastic collisions; Two-phase analysis of ballistic pendulum systems combining momentum conservation and mechanical energy conservation. Excludes: Two-dimensional and oblique collisions (Section 9.5); Center of mass reference frame and multi-particle systems (Section 9.6); Variable-mass systems and rocket propulsion (Section 9.7); Rotational dynamics and angular collisions.

## Learning objectives

1. Distinguish between elastic, inelastic, and perfectly inelastic collisions based on kinetic energy conservation.
2. Apply conservation of linear momentum to solve for final velocities in perfectly inelastic one-dimensional collisions.
3. Apply relative velocity relationships to analyze one-dimensional elastic collisions between two isolated bodies.
4. Predict post-collision motion in elastic collisions for equal masses, stationary targets, and extreme mass ratios.
5. Analyze multi-stage systems like ballistic pendulums by separating collision and mechanical energy swing phases.

## Prerequisites

- `linear-momentum-definition`: Definition of linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity.
- `conservation-of-linear-momentum`: Principle of conservation of linear momentum for an isolated system.
- `translational-kinetic-energy`: Calculation and conceptual understanding of translational kinetic energy.

## Misconception catalogue

- `momentum-lost-in-inelastic`: Believing that total linear momentum is not conserved in inelastic or perfectly inelastic collisions.
- `kinetic-energy-always-conserved`: Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in every collision as long as the colliding objects bounce apart.
- `velocity-sign-neglect`: Treating velocities as scalar magnitudes without assigning algebraic signs for direction in 1D momentum conservation.
- `relative-velocity-formula-misapplication`: Applying the relative velocity relation v1i - v2i = -(v1f - v2f) to inelastic collisions where it is invalid.
- `ballistic-pendulum-energy-conservation`: Assuming mechanical energy is conserved during the inelastic impact phase of a ballistic pendulum.
