# 9.8 Deformable Systems learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.8 Deformable Systems: Distinction between deformable systems and particle models; Failure of the particle work-kinetic energy theorem for deformable systems; Work evaluated using the displacement of the point of application of the force; Distinction between point of application displacement and center-of-mass displacement; Conservation of energy and transformation of internal energy into kinetic energy; Classification of deformable systems as isolated or nonisolated in terms of energy and momentum. Excludes: Rocket propulsion and variable-mass thrust equations; Thermodynamic work and heat transfer in expanding gases; Rotational kinetic energy of extended rigid bodies; Damped and driven harmonic oscillations of spring-mass systems.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain why the particle work-kinetic energy theorem does not generally apply to deformable systems.
2. Distinguish the displacement of a force's application point from the center-of-mass displacement.
3. Apply energy conservation to track internal energy conversions into kinetic energy in deformable systems.
4. Apply the impulse-momentum theorem to determine the center-of-mass motion of deformable systems.
5. Evaluate the work done on a deformable system using the displacement of the point of application.

## Prerequisites

- `center-of-mass-motion`: Understanding center-of-mass definition and its motion under external forces.
- `impulse-momentum-theorem`: Applying the impulse-momentum theorem to relate external impulse and momentum change.
- `work-definition`: Defining mechanical work through the displacement of the point of application of a force.

## Misconception catalogue

- `work-kinetic-energy-always-applies`: Believing the particle work-kinetic energy theorem, W = Delta K, holds universally for deformable systems.
- `normal-force-does-work-pushing-off`: Believing a rigid surface does work on a jumper or skater whose contact point does not move.
- `force-displacement-equals-center-of-mass-displacement`: Confusing the displacement of a force's application point with the displacement of the system center of mass.
- `internal-forces-change-total-momentum`: Believing internal forces, such as muscular exertion or springs, can directly change total linear momentum.
- `energy-isolation-implies-momentum-isolation`: Assuming a system that is isolated in terms of energy must also be isolated in terms of momentum.
