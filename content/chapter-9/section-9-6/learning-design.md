# 9.6 The Center of Mass learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.6 The Center of Mass: Definition and physical concept of center of mass as the mass-weighted average position; Center of mass calculation for discrete systems of two or more particles in one, two, and three dimensions; Translational motion versus rotation under forces applied through versus away from the center of mass; Continuous mass distribution formulation using integral calculus elements; Use of symmetry axes and symmetry planes to locate the center of mass; Definition of center of gravity and conditions under which it coincides with center of mass; Suspension method using intersecting plumb lines to locate the center of gravity. Excludes: Time derivatives of center of mass yielding system velocity and acceleration (Section 9.7); Total linear momentum and Newton's second law for a system of particles (Section 9.7); Kinetic energy and momentum analysis in glancing two-dimensional elastic collisions (Section 9.9); Rotational inertia and moment of inertia integration; Gravitational field gradients causing center of gravity to diverge from center of mass.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain the center of mass as the mass-weighted average position of a system of particles or an extended body.
2. Predict how unequal mass distribution shifts the center of mass position toward the more massive component.
3. Determine the center of mass location for symmetric objects of uniform density using symmetry principles.
4. Distinguish rotational from pure translational motion when a force is applied at versus away from the center of mass.
5. Relate the center of gravity to the center of mass in a uniform gravitational field.
6. Analyze mass differences between segments when an inhomogeneous object is divided at its center of mass.

## Prerequisites

- `vector-position-and-coordinates`: Understanding position vectors and Cartesian coordinate components in one, two, and three dimensions.
- `mass-and-density-distribution`: Understanding mass distribution and differentiating uniform density from non-uniform density.
- `line-of-action-and-rotation`: Recognizing that the line of action of an applied force determines whether an extended body translates or rotates.

## Misconception catalogue

- `cm-always-at-geometric-center`: Believing that the center of mass always coincides with the geometric center regardless of mass distribution.
- `equal-mass-split-at-cm`: Believing that cutting an object through its center of mass separates it into two pieces of equal mass.
- `force-at-any-point-causes-rotation`: Assuming that applying a single force anywhere on an extended body will always cause it to rotate.
- `cg-differs-from-cm-in-uniform-gravity`: Believing the center of gravity and center of mass are located at different points even in a uniform gravitational field.
- `cm-must-lie-within-material`: Assuming that the center of mass must always reside within the solid physical material of the object.
