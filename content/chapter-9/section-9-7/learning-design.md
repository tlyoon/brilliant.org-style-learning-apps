# 9.7 Systems of Many Particles learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.7 Systems of Many Particles: Velocity and linear momentum of a multi-particle system's center of mass; Acceleration of the center of mass and Newton's second law for systems of particles; Pairwise cancellation of internal forces via Newton's third law; Motion of the center of mass as an equivalent single particle under net external force; Conservation of total linear momentum and constant center-of-mass velocity in isolated systems; Trajectory persistence of the center of mass during projectile fragmentation or explosions. Excludes: Work-kinetic energy theorem limitations for deformable systems (Section 9.8); Internal energy transformations and muscular chemical energy changes (Section 9.8); Continuous mass integration for extended rigid bodies with non-uniform density; Rotational motion, torque, and angular momentum of extended bodies; Relativistic momentum and variable-mass rocket propulsion equations.

## Learning objectives

1. Relate the velocity and total momentum of a multi-particle system to the motion of its center of mass.
2. Explain why internal forces between particles cancel in pairs and do not accelerate the system center of mass.
3. Apply Newton's second law for a system of particles to determine the acceleration of the center of mass.
4. Analyze the conservation of linear momentum and center-of-mass motion for an isolated multi-particle system.
5. Predict the trajectory of a system center of mass during internal fragmentation or explosions.

## Prerequisites

- `prereq-center-of-mass-position`: Understanding the definition and calculation of the center of mass position for a system of particles.
- `prereq-newtons-third-law`: Recognizing that interactive forces between two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
- `prereq-linear-momentum-definition`: Defining linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity for a moving particle.

## Misconception catalogue

- `misc-internal-forces-accelerate-cm`: Believing that internal forces within a system can accelerate its center of mass.
- `misc-individual-motion-equals-cm-motion`: Assuming that every fragment or component in a system must move with the same velocity as the center of mass.
- `misc-cm-alters-trajectory-upon-breakup`: Thinking that an exploding projectile's center of mass abruptly deviates from its parabolic path upon breaking apart.
- `misc-momentum-conservation-requires-zero-velocity`: Believing that system momentum conservation only holds if the center of mass is initially at rest.
- `misc-external-force-location-confusion`: Believing that the acceleration of the center of mass depends on which specific particle experiences an external force.
