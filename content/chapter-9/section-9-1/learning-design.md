# 9.1 Linear Momentum learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.1 Linear Momentum: Definition of linear momentum as p = mv with SI units of kgÂ·m/s; Vector characteristics and Cartesian components of linear momentum; Distinction between linear momentum and kinetic energy; Newton's second law formulated as net force equals time derivative of momentum; Derivation of two-particle isolated momentum constancy from Newton's third law. Excludes: Center of mass motion and collisions in two dimensions; Impulse-momentum theorem calculations and time integrals of force; Detailed rocket propulsion variable-mass thrust equations; Rotational motion and angular momentum; Quantitative collision coefficient of restitution and internal kinetic energy loss.

## Learning objectives

1. Define linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity and identify its vector nature, dimensions, and SI units.
2. Distinguish linear momentum from kinetic energy in terms of vector directionality and energy transformations.
3. State and apply Newton's second law as the time rate of change of linear momentum of a particle.
4. Relate momentum conservation in a two-particle system to Newton's third law action-reaction pairs.
5. Compare the momenta of objects having equal kinetic energies or equal speeds with different masses.

## Prerequisites

- `newtons-third-law`: Understanding Newton's third law that interaction forces between two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
- `vector-components`: Ability to resolve two-dimensional and three-dimensional vectors into Cartesian orthogonal components.
- `kinetic-energy-concept`: Knowledge of kinetic energy as a scalar quantity proportional to mass and the square of speed.

## Misconception catalogue

- `momentum-is-scalar`: Believing that momentum is a scalar quantity like kinetic energy, ignoring its directional vector nature.
- `equal-ke-means-equal-momentum`: Assuming objects with identical kinetic energies must also have identical magnitudes of linear momentum.
- `force-equals-momentum`: Confusing net force with momentum rather than recognizing force as the time rate of change of momentum.
- `single-particle-momentum-conservation`: Believing that the momentum of an individual particle is conserved during an interaction within an isolated system.
- `momentum-transformation`: Thinking momentum can transform into other physical forms such as potential or internal momentum analogous to energy.
