# 9.3 Analysis Model: Nonisolated System (Momentum) learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.3 Analysis Model: Nonisolated System (Momentum): Definition of impulse as the integral of net external force over time and area under the force-time curve; Impulse-momentum theorem for a particle and nonisolated system; Relationship between time-averaged force and instantaneous force over a time interval; Application of impulse to impact mitigation and safety mechanisms (airbags, crumple zones); The impulse approximation for brief collisions with dominant contact forces; Vector nature of momentum change and impulse compared to scalar energy transfers. Excludes: Detailed analysis of one-dimensional collisions between multiple isolated particles (Section 9.4); Two-dimensional collisions and center of mass dynamics (Sections 9.5-9.7); Numerical calculus integration of non-constant functions; Rotational momentum and angular impulse (Chapter 10).

## Learning objectives

1. Define linear impulse as the time integral of net external force and the area under a force-time graph.
2. Relate impulse to a particle's change in momentum using the impulse-momentum theorem.
3. Distinguish between a time-varying force and its equivalent constant time-averaged force.
4. Explain how extending collision duration reduces average force for a given momentum change in safety devices.
5. Apply the impulse approximation by identifying dominant impulsive contact forces over brief collision intervals.
6. Contrast momentum transfer across a system boundary with energy transfer mechanisms.

## Prerequisites

- `linear-momentum-definition`: Understanding linear momentum as the vector product of an object's mass and velocity.
- `newtons-second-law-momentum-form`: Understanding Newton's second law as the time rate of change of momentum.
- `vector-change-concept`: Ability to calculate vector changes by subtracting initial velocity from final velocity.

## Misconception catalogue

- `impulse-as-intrinsic-property`: Believing that impulse is an intrinsic property possessed by a moving body rather than a measure of momentum transferred by an external force.
- `ignoring-time-in-momentum-change`: Assuming that peak force alone determines momentum change without considering the time interval during which the force acts.
- `rebound-involves-less-impulse`: Thinking that an object that stops suffers a greater change in momentum and impulse than an object that rebounds at similar speeds.
- `cushioning-increases-impact-force`: Believing that cushioning devices reduce momentum change rather than reducing average force by prolonging collision time for a fixed momentum change.
- `confusing-impulse-with-work`: Confusing impulse (force acting over time to change momentum) with work (force acting over spatial displacement to change energy).
