# 9.5 Collisions in Two Dimensions learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 9.5 Collisions in Two Dimensions: Vector component equations for linear momentum conservation in two dimensions; Glancing collisions and angular deflection analysis; Kinetic energy criteria in two-dimensional elastic versus inelastic collisions; Two-dimensional perfectly inelastic collisions where colliding bodies coalesce; Right-angle separation in elastic collisions between identical masses with a stationary target; Coordinate system selection for planar collision problems. Excludes: Center of mass motion and calculations (Section 9.6); Deformable systems and rocket propulsion (Sections 9.7 and 9.8); Three-dimensional collision dynamics beyond a single plane; One-dimensional relative velocity formula applications (Pitfall Prevention 9.4); Rotational motion, angular momentum, and spin effects during collisions.

## Learning objectives

1. Apply momentum conservation along independent perpendicular axes in two-dimensional collisions.
2. Distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions in two dimensions using kinetic energy criteria.
3. Analyze perfectly inelastic collisions in two dimensions where colliding bodies coalesce after impact.
4. Determine glancing angles and speeds for two-dimensional elastic collisions using momentum and energy equations.
5. Deduce that equal-mass elastic glancing collisions with a stationary target yield perpendicular final trajectories.

## Prerequisites

- `linear-momentum-definition`: Definition of linear momentum as the product of mass and velocity vector.
- `vector-components-resolution`: Resolving two-dimensional vectors into orthogonal Cartesian components.
- `one-dimensional-momentum-conservation`: Conservation of linear momentum in an isolated system during one-dimensional collisions.
- `kinetic-energy-and-elasticity`: Distinction between elastic and inelastic collisions based on kinetic energy conservation.

## Misconception catalogue

- `scalar-momentum-addition`: Believing that total momentum in two dimensions can be conserved by simply adding scalar magnitudes rather than vector components.
- `one-dimensional-relative-velocity-in-2d`: Applying the one-dimensional elastic relative velocity relationship directly to two-dimensional collisions.
- `kinetic-energy-vector-components`: Treating kinetic energy as a vector and attempting to conserve kinetic energy separately along perpendicular coordinate axes.
- `transverse-momentum-neglect`: Assuming momentum along an axis perpendicular to initial motion is always zero or independent of glancing deflection angles.
- `universal-perpendicularity-assumption`: Generalizing the 90-degree post-collision angle to all two-dimensional elastic collisions, regardless of target initial motion or unequal masses.
