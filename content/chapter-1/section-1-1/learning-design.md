# Section 1.1 learning design

## Boundary

This pack begins at “1.1 Standards of Length, Mass, and Time” and ends immediately before “1.2 Modeling and Alternative Representations.” It covers measurement standards, SI, length, mass, time, prefixes, fundamental and derived quantities, and qualitative density relationships. Numerical calculations, unit conversions, copied textbook examples, and Section 1.2 are excluded.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain why a measurement standard must be stable, reproducible, accessible, and independent of a particular person or place.
2. Match length, mass, and time with the SI base units metre, kilogram, and second.
3. Recognize that the current SI defines units through fixed constants of nature, including the speed of light, the Planck constant, and the caesium-133 frequency.
4. Distinguish mass from weight and distinguish an instant in time from a time interval.
5. Use SI prefix names as qualitative scale indicators without performing conversions.
6. Classify quantities as fundamental or derived and reason qualitatively about density as mass relative to volume.

## Prerequisites

- `compare-properties`: compare two objects or descriptions using same, greater, smaller, stable, and changing.
- `read-unit-symbols`: recognize that a quantity and its unit are different roles in a measurement statement.
- `ratio-language`: interpret “more per same amount” and “same amount in less space” qualitatively, without arithmetic.

Each activity names a recovery route to one of these prerequisites. Recovery appears only after evidence from the learner's independent first attempt has been preserved.

## Misconception catalogue

- `standard-can-vary-by-person`: a personal body dimension can serve as a universal standard.
- `standard-may-drift`: a standard remains suitable even when it changes over time.
- `unit-is-quantity`: a unit name and the physical quantity it measures are interchangeable.
- `artifact-still-defines-kilogram`: the international prototype cylinder still defines the kilogram.
- `mass-equals-weight`: mass and weight are the same physical quantity.
- `time-equals-duration`: a clock reading and an elapsed interval mean the same thing.
- `prefix-changes-quantity`: changing an SI prefix changes the physical kind of quantity.
- `all-quantities-are-fundamental`: area, speed, volume, and density are base quantities.
- `same-mass-means-same-volume`: objects with the same mass must occupy the same volume.
- `denser-means-more-mass-always`: the denser material must have more total mass regardless of sample size.
- `same-volume-means-same-density`: objects with the same volume must have the same density regardless of mass.
- `less-mass-means-greater-density`: less mass in the same volume means greater density.
- `base-units-interchangeable`: metre, kilogram, and second may be interchanged among base quantities.
- `greater-volume-means-greater-density`: for equal masses, occupying more volume means greater density.
- `defining-constant-mismatch`: current SI defining constants may be matched to base units interchangeably.
- `constant-definition-is-local`: constant-based SI definitions depend on a particular laboratory or location.
- `historical-definition-is-current`: an older artifact- or Earth-based definition remains the current SI definition.

## Evidence intent

The first submitted response is independent evidence. Opening a hint, retrying, or entering prerequisite recovery marks subsequent success as assisted evidence; it never overwrites the first attempt. This pack describes the evidence contract only and does not implement persistence, mastery, or backend services.
