# 8.5 Power learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 8.5 Power: Definition of instantaneous power as the time rate of energy transfer dE/dt; Definition and calculation of average power as work divided by elapsed time; Mechanical power formula P = F · v for a force acting on a moving particle; SI and customary units of power (watt, horsepower) and energy equivalence of the kilowatt-hour; Analysis of power output under uniform velocity versus uniform upward acceleration. Excludes: Rotational power and torque-angular velocity relationships; Electrical circuit power formulations involving potential difference and current; Thermodynamic cycle power and heat engine rate efficiencies; Variable-force path line integrals requiring numerical calculus.

## Learning objectives

1. Define instantaneous and average power as the time rate of energy transfer or work performed.
2. Relate instantaneous mechanical power to the scalar dot product of force and velocity vectors.
3. Distinguish power units like watts and horsepower from energy units like kilowatt-hours and joules.
4. Analyze the mechanical power required for objects in uniform motion versus accelerating motion.

## Prerequisites

- `work-definition`: Understanding work as an energy transfer mechanism resulting from a force acting through a displacement.
- `newtons-second-law`: Applying net force equations to objects moving at constant velocity versus accelerating.
- `vector-dot-product`: Calculating the scalar product of two vectors using magnitudes and the cosine of the included angle.

## Misconception catalogue

- `power-energy-conflation`: Believing higher power automatically means more total work is done, ignoring the duration of the transfer.
- `kwh-is-power-unit`: Assuming the kilowatt-hour measures power because 'watt' is in the name, rather than energy (power multiplied by time).
- `watts-per-second-confusion`: Treating power as needing an explicit per-second division when the watt is already defined as one joule per second.
- `constant-force-constant-power`: Assuming power delivered by a constant force on an accelerating object remains constant instead of scaling with instantaneous speed.
- `symbol-work-watt-confusion`: Confusing the algebraic quantity symbol for work with the SI unit symbol for power.
