# 8.2 Analysis Model: Isolated System (Energy) learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 8.2 Analysis Model: Isolated System (Energy): Definition and criteria for an isolated system for energy with zero energy transfers across the boundary; Conservation of mechanical energy where kinetic and potential energies transform with constant sum; General conservation of energy for isolated systems containing internal energy transformations; Interplay between gravitational potential energy, elastic potential energy, and kinetic energy; Comparison between isolated multi-object systems and nonisolated single-object systems under external forces; Path independence of energy changes when conservative forces transform mechanical energy. Excludes: Microscopic models of sliding kinetic friction and spot-welded contact mechanics; Thermodynamic cycles and macroscopic heat engine formulations; Electromagnetic radiation transfer and quantum photoelectric effect analysis; Time-dependent power ratings and dissipation rate equations.

## Learning objectives

1. Identify whether a chosen system is isolated or nonisolated based on energy transfers across its boundary.
2. Apply conservation of mechanical energy to isolated systems where only conservative forces act.
3. Differentiate between energy transfer across a boundary and energy transformation within an isolated system.
4. Relate kinetic, gravitational potential, and elastic potential energies in multi-component isolated systems.
5. Account for internal energy changes when nonconservative forces act within an isolated system.
6. Analyze how choosing different system boundaries alters whether interactions are internal potential energy or external work.

## Prerequisites

- `prereq-kinetic-energy`: Understanding kinetic energy as energy associated with the state of motion of an object.
- `prereq-potential-energy`: Understanding potential energy as stored energy dependent on the spatial configuration of interacting members.
- `prereq-work-energy-relation`: Understanding work as an energy transfer across a system boundary caused by a force acting through a displacement.

## Misconception catalogue

- `misc-single-object-potential-energy`: Believing that a single particle can possess potential energy on its own without including interacting objects in the system.
- `misc-conserved-means-individual-energies-constant`: Believing that no internal forces can do work inside an isolated system to transform energy among kinetic, potential, and internal forms.
- `misc-mechanical-energy-always-conserved`: Assuming mechanical energy is conserved even when nonconservative forces act internally to convert mechanical energy into internal energy.
- `misc-path-dependence-in-conservative-systems`: Believing that the final speed of an object in a conservative isolated system depends on the trajectory path rather than the endpoint configurations.
