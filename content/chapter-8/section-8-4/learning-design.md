# 8.4 Changes in Mechanical Energy for Nonconservative Forces learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 8.4 Changes in Mechanical Energy for Nonconservative Forces: Equation of energy conservation for isolated systems with nonconservative forces: ΔK + ΔU + ΔEint = 0; Transformation of mechanical energy into internal energy via kinetic friction (ΔEint = fk * d); Systems with combined gravitational potential energy and friction (e.g., sliding down inclines); Systems with combined elastic potential energy and friction (e.g., block-spring collisions with friction); Interpretation of energy bar charts showing conversion between kinetic, potential, and internal energies; Effect of sliding distance and friction coefficient on energy dissipation. Excludes: Power and rate of energy transfer (Section 8.5); Conservative forces and potential energy functions or gradients (-dU/dx); Non-mechanical energy transfers such as heat conduction, electromagnetic radiation, or chemical reactions; Air resistance and drag forces with speed dependence; Thermodynamics and temperature rise calculations from internal energy changes.

## Learning objectives

1. Apply the energy conservation equation ΔK + ΔU + ΔEint = 0 to isolated systems involving kinetic friction.
2. Relate the increase in internal energy ΔEint to the work done against kinetic friction (fk * d).
3. Analyze systems combining gravitational, elastic potential, and internal energy changes.
4. Interpret energy bar charts tracking kinetic, potential, internal, and constant total energy in an isolated system.
5. Predict how changing path length or surface friction affects final speed and mechanical energy dissipation.

## Prerequisites

- `kinetic-and-potential-energy-definitions`: Understanding kinetic energy (K) and potential energies (gravitational Ug and elastic Us).
- `friction-and-normal-force`: Calculating kinetic friction force using the coefficient of kinetic friction and normal force (fk = μk * n).
- `isolated-system-energy-conservation`: Applying the work-energy theorem and the concept of an isolated system where total energy is conserved.

## Misconception catalogue

- `friction-destroys-energy`: Believing that friction permanently destroys mechanical energy rather than converting it into internal energy.
- `normal-force-always-equals-mg`: Assuming the normal force always equals mg, regardless of incline angle or surface orientation.
- `mechanical-energy-conservation-with-friction`: Assuming mechanical energy (K + U) remains conserved even when nonconservative forces like kinetic friction are present.
- `friction-work-independent-of-path-length`: Thinking that energy converted by friction depends only on initial and final positions, ignoring total sliding distance.
- `internal-energy-can-decrease-spontaneously`: Assuming ΔEint due to kinetic friction can be negative during sliding motion.
