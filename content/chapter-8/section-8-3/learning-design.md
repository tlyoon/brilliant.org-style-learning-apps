# 8.3 Situations Involving Kinetic Friction learning design

## Boundary

Includes concepts supported by the controlled PDF for 8.3 Situations Involving Kinetic Friction: Microscopic model of kinetic friction and point-of-application displacement differences; Limitations of the particle work-kinetic energy theorem for sliding deformable interfaces; Transformation of kinetic energy into system internal energy through friction; Energy conservation formulation for nonisolated systems with friction dissipation; Effect of applied pulling angle on normal force, kinetic friction, and final kinetic energy; Horizontal spring-mass motion with kinetic friction retarding forces. Excludes: Gravitational potential energy changes on rough inclines (covered in Section 8.4); General potential energy functions and conservative force gradient definitions; Static friction thresholds, impending motion, and rolling without slipping; Fluid drag forces and terminal velocity dynamics.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain why the work done by kinetic friction cannot be calculated from object displacement alone.
2. Relate the increase in internal energy of a system to the kinetic friction force and path length.
3. Apply energy conservation equations to nonisolated systems experiencing friction dissipation.
4. Analyze how force angle alters normal force, kinetic friction, and kinetic energy gain.
5. Describe microscopic contact and deformation mechanisms transforming kinetic energy into internal energy.

## Prerequisites

- `work-and-kinetic-energy`: Understanding work done by external forces and its relation to changes in kinetic energy for a particle.
- `kinetic-friction-and-normal-force`: Understanding the kinetic friction model where frictional magnitude is proportional to the normal force.
- `elastic-potential-energy-springs`: Understanding the work done by a spring on a moving mass and energy storage in compressed springs.

## Misconception catalogue

- `friction-work-equals-force-times-distance`: Believing that the work done by kinetic friction can be calculated simply as force times object displacement, ignoring microscopic interface deformation.
- `friction-destroys-energy`: Assuming that mechanical energy lost to friction disappears entirely rather than transforming into internal energy and mechanical waves.
- `horizontal-pull-always-maximizes-speed`: Assuming an applied pulling force must be strictly horizontal to maximize acceleration, neglecting that an upward angle reduces normal force and friction.
- `internal-energy-confined-to-sliding-object`: Believing that all internal energy generated by friction enters the sliding object alone rather than being shared across both surfaces.
- `friction-displacement-identical-to-bulk-motion`: Assuming the point of application of friction moves through the exact displacement of the moving object, overlooking localized microscopic shearing.
