## Evidence checked
- Equal-mass sticking: momentum-conserving shared forward velocity is half initial velocity; half of the original kinetic energy remains.
- Equal-mass elastic exchange: the initially moving object can stop as the equal-mass target takes its velocity; an inelastic sticking counterexample disproves unconditional exchange.
- Newton's cradle: the proposed two outgoing balls, each at half speed, conserve momentum but have only half the kinetic energy needed for an elastic collision.
- Extreme mass ratio, perfectly inelastic embedding: final velocity and retained kinetic-energy fraction both approach zero as target mass increases.
- Ballistic pendulum: conserve momentum during embedding, lose kinetic energy, and separately conserve mechanical energy during the rise.
- Ideal spring compression: spring potential increases as the two-block kinetic energy decreases, with total momentum conserved.

## Extension: remaining three multiple-choice physics claims

- Inelastic but *not* perfectly inelastic collision. For restitution e=1/2, m1=2, m2=3, and initial speed 5, exact momentum is conserved and total kinetic energy is 11/20 of initial energy; the bodies do not stick.
